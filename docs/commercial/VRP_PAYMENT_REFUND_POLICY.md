## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the proposed payment, cancellation, refund, and financial settlement principles for commercial engagements involving Veil Routing Protocol (VRP).

VRP is independently developed networking and session continuity technology.

Its fundamental architectural principle is:

**SESSION ≠ TRANSPORT**

VRP commercial engagements may involve specialized engineering work, controlled technical evaluations, runtime licensing, integration services, validation programs, and strategic licensing arrangements.

These activities may require advance allocation of engineering resources, infrastructure, development capacity, and confidential technical assets.

The objective of this policy is to establish transparent commercial expectations before work begins.

This document is a proposed B2B policy.

It does not independently create a binding contract or payment obligation.

The terms of an executed agreement and applicable mandatory law take precedence.

---

## 2. Commercial Principles

VRP commercial transactions are intended to follow these principles:

1. Transparent pricing.
2. Defined contractual deliverables.
3. Written payment schedules.
4. Controlled engineering resource allocation.
5. Clear cancellation procedures.
6. Protection of completed engineering work.
7. Fair treatment of material non-performance.
8. Documented acceptance criteria.
9. No automatic entitlement to unrestricted technology access.
10. Contractual accountability for both parties.

Commercial payments are consideration for specifically agreed licensing rights, services, deliverables, or reserved resources.

Payment does not transfer ownership of VRP intellectual property unless expressly agreed.

## 17. Technical Evaluation Outcomes

A technical evaluation may produce different outcomes.

### PASS

The agreed acceptance criteria were satisfied under recorded test conditions.

### PARTIAL

Some criteria were satisfied while others remain unresolved.

### FAIL

One or more agreed acceptance criteria were not satisfied.

### INCONCLUSIVE

Available evidence is insufficient to establish a reliable verdict.

A failed or inconclusive evaluation does not automatically establish entitlement to a full refund.

Equally, a failed evaluation does not automatically permit retention of all fees.

Financial consequences depend on the agreed scope, service obligations, acceptance criteria, contractual remedies, and applicable law.

The parties should distinguish between:

- Payment for a good-faith technical evaluation.
- Payment conditional on achieving specified technical results.
- Payment for defined deliverables.
- Payment for production licensing rights.

These arrangements may have different refund consequences.
