## 1. Executive Statement

Veil Routing Protocol (VRP) is independently developed networking and session continuity technology.

Its foundational architectural principle is:

**SESSION ≠ TRANSPORT**

VRP is designed to investigate and implement selected mechanisms for logical session identity, transport independence, authority-aware state management, and controlled recovery.

Enterprise adoption of specialized networking technology requires clear contractual treatment of warranties, liability, technical limitations, and operational risks.

This document establishes the proposed warranty and liability framework for VRP commercial engagements.

It is intended to support transparent negotiations between the lawful VRP rights holder and prospective enterprise customers.

This document does not independently create binding contractual terms.

All enforceable rights, warranties, remedies, exclusions, and limitations must be established in an applicable executed agreement and remain subject to mandatory law.

## 2. Fundamental Legal Principles

The proposed VRP commercial model follows these principles:

1. Contractual clarity.
2. Defined technical obligations.
3. Evidence-based acceptance.
4. Explicit allocation of operational responsibilities.
5. Protection of intellectual property.
6. Proportionate allocation of commercial risk.
7. Transparent disclosure of known material limitations.

## 4. Technical Capability Classification

VRP technical capabilities should be classified according to available engineering evidence.

### VERIFIED

A capability supported by reproducible validation under identified conditions.

### IN DEVELOPMENT

## 6. No Universal Continuity Warranty

VRP is developed around session continuity principles.

However, no general warranty is made that:

- Network connectivity will never fail.
- Every session will survive every outage.
- All transport migrations will succeed.
- Packet loss will be eliminated.
- Application transactions will never be interrupted.
- Every recovery operation will complete.
- Every network environment will be compatible.
- Every distributed state conflict will be prevented.

Continuity behavior depends on the implementation, integration, infrastructure, and failure conditions.

Any binding continuity commitment must be explicitly defined and technically measurable.
