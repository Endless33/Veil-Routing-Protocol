## 1. Executive Overview

Veil Routing Protocol (VRP) is independently developed networking and session continuity technology.

Its fundamental architectural principle is:

**SESSION ≠ TRANSPORT**

VRP is designed around the separation of logical session identity from individual network transport paths.

The technology is intended for specialized engineering evaluation and selectively authorized commercial use.

Enterprise engagement with VRP follows a controlled process intended to establish:

- Technical relevance
- Commercial eligibility
- Defined evaluation objectives
- Appropriate confidentiality
- Clear licensing rights
- Measurable acceptance criteria
- Engineering feasibility
- Transparent financial obligations
- Evidence-based technical decisions
- Controlled production authorization

This document describes the proposed process for initiating and conducting an enterprise engagement.

It does not constitute a binding offer, guarantee of acceptance, or commitment to provide a particular service.

## 2. Engagement Philosophy

VRP enterprise engagements follow five principles.

### 2.1 Technical Substance

Commercial discussions should begin with a clearly defined engineering problem.

### 2.2 Selective Participation

Not every proposed engagement will be technically or commercially suitable.

### 2.3 Controlled Disclosure

Protected implementation assets remain subject to intellectual property and confidentiality restrictions.

### 2.4 Evidence-Based Evaluation

Technical conclusions should be supported by reproducible validation.

### 2.5 Separate Production Authorization

Evaluation success does not automatically establish production deployment rights.

## 3. Enterprise Engagement Lifecycle

The proposed engagement lifecycle is:

**INQUIRY → QUALIFICATION → NDA → TECHNICAL SCOPING → PROPOSAL → AGREEMENT → PAYMENT → EVALUATION → EVIDENCE REVIEW → ACCEPTANCE → PRODUCTION DECISION**

Each stage has a distinct purpose.

Progression is subject to technical feasibility, commercial agreement, and available engineering capacity.

Stages may be combined, modified, or omitted where appropriate and expressly agreed.

## 21. Evaluation Verdicts

Formal evaluation outcomes should use defined classifications.

### PASS

All mandatory acceptance criteria for the evaluated scope were satisfied under recorded conditions.

### PARTIAL

Some criteria were satisfied while others remain unresolved.

### FAIL

One or more mandatory acceptance criteria were not satisfied.

### INCONCLUSIVE

Available evidence is insufficient to establish a reliable verdict.

### NOT TESTED

A criterion was not executed.

### BLOCKED

A required prerequisite prevented evaluation.

Verdicts must reflect actual observations.

No PASS verdict should be assigned without supporting evidence.
